\begin{abstract}
We study a frozen coordinate metric ansatz motivated by special-holonomy
neck geometry. A deterministic pipeline separates numerical approximations,
validated scalar eigenvalue bounds, and unresolved geometric hypotheses.
The scalar interval certificate is relative to the supplied coordinate
coefficients. Existence of a nearby torsion-free structure, identification
with a global K3 fibre metric, and the proposed matching multiplicities
remain open. This provisional abstract will be replaced after the
submission gates close.
\end{abstract}

\section{Coordinate model}
The coefficient space is
$\mathbb R^{\Claim{parameter_count}}$.
A raw polynomial Cholesky field is passed through diagonal softplus,
then scaled to impose $\det g=\Claim{determinant}$.
The diagonal is positive, so the resulting real coordinate metric is
positive definite. The domain is
$[\Claim{left_endpoint},\Claim{right_endpoint}]$.
The polynomial seam is extended by exponential tails matching its
value and first derivative.
This construction specifies a matrix field; the global K3 input and
its relation to a torsion-free $G_{\Claim{two}}$ structure require
separate evidence.

\section{Metric certificate and adiabatic transfer}
Only the raw Cholesky field belongs to a finite polynomial space.
Softplus and determinant normalization make the reconstructed metric
non-polynomial in general. A finite interpolation calculation therefore
needs a remainder bound.

The historical derivative from a finite coefficient space into the
stated space of continuous functions cannot possess the claimed
two-sided inverse. A replacement Newton--Kantorovich theorem must specify
a square residual or a compatible right-inverse framework, the gauge,
the norm, the boundary constraints and the certified neighbourhood.
The present manuscript does not assert torsion-free existence.
The adiabatic bound for the full metric remains pending.

\section{Reduced scalar spectrum}
Define $a(s)=g^{ss}(s)$ and the Neumann operator
$Au=-(a u')'$ on the frozen interval. The constant determinant makes
the scalar weight constant. A conservative mass-lumped finite-element
calculation gives the finite-grid first positive eigenvalue
$\lambda_{\mathrm{grid}}=\Claim{scalar_gap}$.
The artifact records grid refinement, the discrete residual, and a
Richardson estimate; the grid value is not an enclosure.

\begin{proposition}[Reduced scalar enclosure]
Let $a_-$ and $a_+$ bound the positive coefficient everywhere on an
interval of length $L$. The first positive Neumann eigenvalue satisfies
\[
 a_-\frac{\pi^{\Claim{two}}}{L^{\Claim{two}}}
 \leq\lambda_{\mathrm{first}}\leq
 a_+\frac{\pi^{\Claim{two}}}{L^{\Claim{two}}}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On functions of zero mean, the interval Poincare inequality bounds
the Rayleigh quotient below using $a_-$. The mean-zero cosine test
function gives the upper bound using $a_+$. The Neumann kernel consists
of constants, so the variational characterization applies to the first
positive eigenvalue.
\end{proof}
Directed interval reconstruction of the coefficient, with rational
coverage and endpoint checks, gives
\[
 \lambda_{\mathrm{first}}\in
 [\Claim{lambda_lower},\Claim{lambda_upper}].
\]
The certificate concerns the exact dyadic input and this reduced scalar
operator. It is not an interval for the full Hodge Laplacian of an
unknown torsion-free metric.

We use only the counting-function convention
$N(\lambda)\sim C\lambda^\alpha$, with $\alpha=d/\Claim{two}$.
The one-dimensional reference is $\Claim{weyl_one_dimensional}$,
whereas the seven-dimensional reference is $\Claim{weyl_seven_dimensional}$.
The recorded finite-window slope is $\Claim{weyl_alpha}$;
fit error and window sensitivity are provided separately.
This diagnostic is not a proof of an asymptotic law.
The full Kaluza--Klein tower is pending: a K3 spectrum, its multiplicities
and certified tail bounds must replace the historical integer-momentum
model for the fibre.

\section{Profiles, matching and pairing}
For the usual cohomology of the bare product neck, the Kunneth calculation
gives dimensions $\Claim{product_h_two}$ and $\Claim{product_h_three}$

The scalar interpolation equation $-(p f')'=\Claim{zero}$ is solved

$\Claim{profile_error}$ at the finest reported degree.
These are radial interpolation profiles. Global harmonic representatives,
their independence and completeness have not been established.
The proposed geometric intersection pairing remains pending because
the matching-selected lattice basis is missing.

\section{Product sector identity}
\begin{proposition}
On an exact product with a constant-radius circle, let $\vartheta$
be its parallel one-form and let $f$ depend only on the base.
With corresponding radial boundary conditions,
\[
 \Delta_{\Claim{one}}(f\vartheta)
   =(\Delta_{\Claim{zero}}f)\vartheta .
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The product Laplacian splits into the factor Laplacians.
The circle one-form is parallel and harmonic, and the mixed derivatives
vanish. Restricting to base-dependent scalar functions gives the identity.
\end{proof}
This identifies selected sectors and does not equate full spectra with
multiplicity. The fresh coordinate block projection gives relative gap
difference $\Claim{democracy_deviation}$.
No theorem identifying the additional form sector is asserted.
