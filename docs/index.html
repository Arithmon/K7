The K₇ Framework

What if physics isn't fine-tuned, just well-shaped?

K₇ assigns 33 dimensionless parameters of the Standard Model and of cosmology to closed forms in the invariants of a single 7-dimensional shape. It is a declared correspondence, not a derivation: what was set is written down, and so is the rule under which each relation can fail.

Part of the Arithmon program — the hypothesis that the constants of nature are counts.

Where It Stands

Thirty-three relations with no continuously adjustable parameter, compared with the most recent data (PDG 2026, NuFIT 6.1, Planck 2018, CODATA 2022). The formulas are assigned, not derived, and the classes below are not added into a single score.

The 33 relations in five classes

Three sectors are overdetermined: four lepton relations bear on two mass ratios, three CKM relations on two parameters, seven density relations on two densities. In each, the relations disagree with one another as exact equalities, and none is selected after the fact. The 33 are not 33 independent tests.

Is the agreement distinctive?

No. In a preregistered look-elsewhere test, every competitor searches for its own formulas, in the same grammar and with the same budget.

Test Result
Targets reached by K₇ 31 of 33
Alternative Betti pairs that reach as many 1 085 of 4 159 (P = 0.26)
Invented vocabularies that reach as many 1 015 of 2 000 (P = 0.51)
A control with no K₇ constant 30 of 33
Preregistered verdict look-elsewhere effect not overcome

Closeness to the measured values is therefore not, by itself, evidence for K₇. The coincidence probabilities and the P-values shown on earlier versions of this page are withdrawn. What remains is rigidity (an integer ratio cannot be nudged, so one measurement can reject a relation) and a written list of everything that was set rather than derived.

Decision Rule

The relations are stated as exact equalities. A relation is rejected when its reference series (PDG, NuFIT, Planck, CODATA) excludes its value at 3σ. The rule has no repair clause: a relation that fails is not rescued by changing its formula, its unit, its scale or its dataset.

Relation Value Rejected when Where
δCP, the phase 2π·197/360 197° a global fit whose 3σ interval excludes 197°; the unit assignment is rejected with it DUNE, Hyper-Kamiokande
sin²θ23 6/11 a global fit whose 3σ interval excludes 6/11, in either octant DUNE, Hyper-Kamiokande
sin2θ12 4/13 a global fit whose 3σ interval excludes 4/13 JUNO (first result 0.3092 ± 0.0087; about four times tighter than today’s global fit after six years)
mτ/me and QKoide 1776.74 and 1776.97 MeV for mτ an mτ value whose 3σ interval excludes the value, each relation separately Belle II, BES III
Ngen 3 a fourth sequential generation LHC, FCC
ms/md 20 a lattice determination with an uncertainty whose 3σ interval excludes 20 Lattice QCD

Standing verdicts. Six relations are already rejected as exact equalities and kept only as numerical proximities: α−1, sin²θW, mμ/me, mμ/mτ, mb/mt and mW/mZ. sin²θ23 is in tension: the NuFIT 6.1 best fit sits in the lower octant, and 6/11 lies inside its 3σ range. Two contradicting measurements outside the reference series are reported beside their class: h = 167/248 agrees with Planck and is excluded by the SH0ES distance ladder at 5.5σ; the density relations agree with Planck and are strained by the baryon acoustic oscillations of DESI (second data release): about 2σ with DESI alone, and −3.3σ, +3.4σ and −3.1σ for ΩΛ/Ωm, Ωm/ΩΛ and ΩDE with DESI and the cosmic microwave background combined (−3.0σ for h). A rejection rejects a relation and the assignment that produced it, not the programme by itself. Every verdict is recorded, favourable or not, in the program scoreboard.
